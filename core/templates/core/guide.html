How it works

Two minutes, then you can ignore this forever.

Parts don't get used up. They get held.
A project borrows parts from your bin and keeps them until you tear it down. Then most of them come back, minus the ones soldered into the board and the ones that died.

That means every part has three numbers, and only the first one is stored:

1. Get your parts in

The fast way is Import: paste a list, one part per line. Name and quantity are all that's required. 

10k resistor, 180, 10k, through-hole, passive, resistor
DHT22, 4
ESP32 devkit, 2, , module, mcu, wifi

The fields are name, quantity, value, package, then tags. Anything after the fourth comma becomes a tag, so tags can contain commas. If one line is malformed nothing is imported and you're told which line, so you can't end up half done without knowing it.

Or use Add part and hit Save and add another, which puts you back on an empty form with the cursor in the name field.

2. Start a build

Make a project, then add the parts it needs. Allocating doesn't change what you own, only what's available, because the parts are still yours. They're just spoken for.

3. When you haven't got enough

Asking for six when you have two isn't an error. You get the two, and the other four are recorded as short. Short parts appear on the shopping list on the Bench, totalled per part across all your live builds, because you buy per part rather than per project.

You can also put something on the list without a project at all. Open a part's page and set Want to buy. That's for "I'm running low on 10k resistors", which has nothing to do with any particular build. Both sources add up on one row, and a delivery takes it back off.

4. Changed your mind mid-build

Return on a project line hands parts straight back to the shelf without ending the project. Remove deletes a line you never actually pulled parts for.

5. Tear it down

This is the part that matters. When a build is finished or scrapped, hit Tear down and say what became of every part:

Every line has to add up exactly to what the project was holding, with no leftovers. Everything is pre-filled as returned, so a clean teardown is one click. Soldered and broken are the only two ways a part ever leaves your inventory.

The project is then archived. It keeps its parts list forever, so you can look back at what a build was made of and what it cost you.

Got it wrong? Archived projects have a Reopen button. It puts the project back on the bench and returns everything the teardown wrote off. Parts you handed back during the build stay handed back, because only the teardown itself is reversed.

Where a number came from

Every change to a quantity is recorded: opening balance, delivery, recount, and what a teardown consumed. A part's page shows the whole run, with the balance after each one, so "that number looks wrong" is a question with an answer rather than a shrug.

Where things live

Tags

Tags group things the columns can't: i2c, 3v3, salvaged. Click any tag to filter the parts list to it, and the add form autocompletes tags you already use, so a typo doesn't quietly become a second tag one character away from the first.

Tags lists them all with counts, and lets you rename one across every part at once, or remove it. A part's page also shows others sharing its tags under Might do instead, which is as close to a substitutes list as this app pretends to get: whether one actually substitutes is your call.

Two rows for one component

10k, 10 K and 10kΩ are one resistor to you and three rows to a database, and your count ends up split across all three. The importer refuses near-duplicates, a part's page warns you when it spots one, and Duplicates lists every group it can find.

Merge folds one into the other: quantities add up, project lines combine, and the history carries over. 4.7k and 47k are correctly left alone.

Two things worth knowing

Bought more? Use Add stock on the part's page. It adds to what you own so you don't do the arithmetic yourself. Fixing a miscount is different: edit the part and set the real number.

Numbers drifted? They will. Count what's actually in the bin and edit the part. The app can't stop you being wrong, only make being right cheap.
